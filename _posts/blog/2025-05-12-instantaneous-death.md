title: The Idea of Instantaneous Death Is So Hard to Comprehend, Because It Doesn't Exist

When we talk about death, we often imagine it as a moment, a single, final instant in time when life ceases and something else begins. We picture it happening in a flash, as if a switch is flipped. But the more you reflect on the nature of consciousness, the more you realize that **instantaneous death is a concept that only makes sense from within the physical world**. Outside of it, the experience is something entirely different.

When transitioning from the physical plane to the infinite, spiritual, or non-physical realm, the perception of time changes completely. What seems instantaneous to someone fully grounded in the physical world might appear to stretch over a millennium to someone whose awareness has already begun to separate from the material.

This is not about a stopwatch or a ticking clock. It is about **awareness**, and the way it interacts with what we call time. Physical time is linear, measurable, and absolute in appearance. But that structure only applies within the physical plane. The spiritual or non-physical realm is not bound by it. Time, in that state, is felt differently. It can expand or contract depending on your state of attachment, clarity, and presence.

The more deeply you are attached to the physical world, the longer the process of letting go feels, and the longer your death seems to take. Clinging to identity, possessions, body, or fear creates a kind of gravity that holds the soul in the space between here and what lies beyond. In contrast, if you are already free from physical attachments, if your inner being is already untethered, **the transition can be as effortless as taking off a shirt or walk thru a open door**.

This is why the idea of instantaneous death is so difficult to comprehend. It does not truly exist in the way we imagine it. Death is not a single moment. It is a passage. And how long that passage feels depends not on the clock, but on the soul.


A profound image of such a transition is in the verse:

> **"וַיְהִי בְּעֶצֶם הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה, הוֹצִיא יְהוָה אֶת־בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל מֵאֶרֶץ מִצְרַיִם עַל־צִבְאֹתָם."**  
> (שמות פרק י״ב פסוק נ״א)

_"And it came to pass on that very day, the Lord brought the Children of Israel out of the land of Egypt by their divisions."_

This was not merely a historical departure. Egypt—*Mitzrayim*, from the root *metzarim*, "narrow places"—is a symbol of spiritual constriction and physical limitation. The exodus is a metaphor for the soul's release from bondage. And just like the exodus, **this release happens not randomly, but with purpose, structure, and divine timing**.

"On that very day" shows that transitions like these are real, anchored in sacred timing. "By their divisions" reminds us that souls do not leave haphazardly, but in alignment with who they are, what they carry, and where they are going.

Understanding this can shift the way we live. If we carry less attachment, less fear, and more openness to the non-physical, death becomes less of a terrifying endpoint and more of a natural unfolding. It becomes a return, not an end. In that sense, the true preparation for death is not found in medicine or ritual. It begins with learning to loosen our grip on what we believe defines us here.
